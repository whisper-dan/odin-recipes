Beans Pottage

Description

Beans pottage/porridge is pretty much parboiled beans cooked in a tomato sauce. Nothing special about it, but it tastes good when cooked right. In the author's house, beans pottage is simply called "beans". It isn't eaten any other way! Beans pottage has a special place in his heart (mouth?(, so try this recipe and see if you like it too!

Ingredients

  1. Honey Beans (Ewa Oloyin)
  2. Water
  3. Tomatoes
  4. Fresh Tomato + Fresh Pepper Paste (purchase separately and blend together, then boil)
  5. Tomato Paste (From the store, should be dark and thick, may contain pepper and onions)
  6. Onions
  7. Curry, Thyme, and Other Spices
  8. Palm Oil
  9. Salt
  10. Stock Cubes
  11. Stock

Steps

  1. Get all ingredients ready (boil the beans with some salt, make sure the stock and tomato+pepper paste aren't frozen, slice tomatoes and onions).
  2. Heat oil in a pan and fry onions in it till it's golden brown.
  3. While frying onions, add all spices to the oil. This will make the food taste much better.
  4. Add tomato paste (store-bought) to the fried onions.
  5. Mix it around.
  6. Add sliced tomatoes and let them cook a bit
  7. Add your tomato+pepper paste to the pot, to the desired level of spicyness.
  8. At this point wait until the taste changes, the bitterness of the sliced tomato should turn sweet
  9. Add stock to the pot and wait for it to heat up.
  10. Taste the stock. If it's not spicy or salty enough, add salt, tomato+pepper paste or stock cubes as appropriate, keeping in mind that the beans is (are?) unseasoned and will dilute whatever you can taste right now.
  11. Add beans
  12. Add enough water to cover the beans. At this point, be careful, if there isn't enough water you can add more later, but if there's too much it's harder to fix.
  13. Let it cook.
  14. Once the beans is soft enough, turn off the fire.

Extra Info

  • If there is too much liquid in your beans pottage, lower the heat to let evaporation occur. Maybe add raw potatoes to soak it up.
  • Note that the liquid part of the beans pottage will thicken when it cools, so don't fret too much about it.

Try it out and enjoy. Beans pottage goes well with 'garri' (cassava flakes mixed with water and sugar) and/or fried plantain slices, but you could genuinely pair it with anything.
